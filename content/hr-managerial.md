> [!abstract] What these rounds test
> **Managerial / techno-managerial** (usually a senior manager): your project, **"what would you do if…"** scenarios, trade-offs, attitude and communication. **HR:** motivation, stability, CTC, notice period, location, flexibility. On 17 Oct all rounds happen on the **same day**, so keep energy for the last one.

## Frameworks to remember

> [!question]- Q1. What is STAR and how do I use it?
> **In simple words:** a 4-step way to tell any "tell me about a time…" story so it's short and clear.
>
> | Step | Say | Time |
> |---|---|---|
> | **S**ituation | where / when, one sentence | 10 s |
> | **T**ask | what needed doing, *your* responsibility | 10 s |
> | **A**ction | what **I** did, 2–3 concrete steps | 40 s |
> | **R**esult | outcome, **a number** if possible, what you learned | 15 s |
>
> **Diagram:**
> ```mermaid
> flowchart LR
>   S["Situation<br/>set the scene"] --> T["Task<br/>your goal"] --> A["Action<br/>what I did"] --> R["Result<br/>number + lesson"]
> ```
> 💡 **Hack:** *"Set the scene, Target, Actions, Reward."* If you're rambling, **jump to the result**.

> [!question]- Q2. How do I answer "What would you do if…" questions?
> **In simple words:** use **CARE** so you never freeze:
>
> 1. **C**larify: ask 1 question or state your assumption ("How many users are affected?")
> 2. **A**nalyse impact: severity, deadline, who is affected
> 3. **R**espond: act **and communicate** (lead, client, team)
> 4. **E**valuate / prevent: root cause, test, process change
>
> 💡 **Hack:** *"Show them you CARE."* Managers love **communicate early** and **prevent next time**.

> [!question]- Q3. Your STAR story bank (reuse for many questions)
> Prepare these once; each one answers several questions.
>
> | Story | Use it for |
> |---|---|
> | **Step Functions pipeline, 80–90% faster** | biggest achievement, challenge, initiative, new technology |
> | **Test coverage 20% → 78%** | quality, ownership, legacy code |
> | **AI spec agent with MCP** | innovation, learning, going beyond your role |
> | **SCA child-dependency fix** | problem-solving, when the obvious fix fails |
> | **Dynamic Targets go-live** | teamwork, stakeholders, deadlines, cross-team conflict |
> | ✍️ **A real mistake / production issue** | failure, pressure, lesson learned |

## HR questions

> [!question]- Q4. Why do you want to leave Accenture? ⭐

>
> > "Accenture has been great for me: I got broad exposure across front end, back end and cloud, and recognition with the Pinnacle Award and a promotion. After 4.5 years I'm looking for **larger-scale client projects and more ownership of architecture**, and a fresh environment to grow faster."
>
> 💡 **Hack:** *"I'm running **towards** something, not away from something."*

> [!question]- Q5. Why Infosys? ⭐

>
> > "Three reasons. **Scale:** Infosys works with large global clients across banking, retail and manufacturing, so I'd work on bigger systems. **Learning:** the culture of continuous learning, Infosys Springboard and Lex, and its AI and cloud platforms like **Topaz** and **Cobalt**. **Fit:** this role is Angular, TypeScript and full stack, which is exactly my strength, so I can contribute from day one."

> 💡 **Hack:** **"Scale, Skills, Strength."**

> [!question]- Q7. Why should we hire you? ⭐

> 💡 **Hack:** **always "three reasons"**. It sounds structured and confident.

> [!question]- Q8. What is your current and expected CTC?
> **In simple words:** give **real numbers**, a **justification**, and stay **calm and firm**. Never say "as per company norms" for a lateral role.
>
> > "My current CTC is **[X] LPA**. I'm expecting **[Y] LPA**, based on my 4.5 years, full-stack skills across Angular, Node, .NET and AWS, and the promotion with the Pinnacle Award. I'm open to discussing the overall package."
>
> - You already shared your numbers with the recruiter on 14 Oct. **Stay consistent with them.**
> - If they push lower: ask about **joining bonus, variable pay, level / designation**, and say you'll consider the full offer.
>
> 💡 **Hack:** **number → reason → "open to the full package".** Silence after a number is fine. Don't fill it by lowering yourself.

> [!question]- Q9. What is your notice period? Can you join earlier?
> > "My notice period is **[X] days**. I'll request an early release, and I'm open to a buyout if Infosys supports it."
>
> ✍️ Know your exact notice period and the earliest realistic date. Only promise what is true.

> [!question]- Q10. Are you open to relocation, work from office, or shifts? ⭐
> Infosys candidates often report questions about **flexibility: location, office days, extended hours**. Decide your answer **before** you walk in.
>
> > "Yes. I'm open to [Bangalore / Pune / Nagpur DC, whichever you prefer] and to the hybrid policy. For occasional extended hours around releases or client overlap, that's fine."
>
> 💡 **Hack:** **"Flexible, with a preference."** Saying the preference is fine; refusing outright isn't.

> [!question]- Q11. Do you have other offers?
> > "I'm in process with a couple of companies, but Infosys is a strong preference because of the role fit."
>
> Don't name companies unless asked. Don't bluff an offer you don't have.

> [!question]- Q12. Would you be okay working on a technology you haven't used (e.g. Java or React)?
> > "Yes. I learn by building. I picked up Step Functions and MCP that way. My fundamentals in JavaScript, TypeScript and APIs transfer well. I'd ask for a short ramp-up and a good first ticket."
>
> 💡 **Hack:** **"Yes + proof I learn fast + plan."**

> [!question]- Q13. Are you comfortable with a service-company environment and client interaction?
> > "Yes. At Accenture I already work in a client-delivery model: sprint ceremonies, status updates, and coordinating with other teams like Pricing and Solutioning for the Dynamic Targets go-live."

> [!question]- Q14. Do you have any questions for us? (always say yes)
> **Technical / managerial round:**
> 1. "What project or client would this role be on, and what's the Angular version and stack?"
> 2. "What does success look like in the first 6 months?"
> 3. "How is the team structured?"
>

>
> 💡 **Hack:** never ask about leave or salary in the technical round. Save money talk for HR.

## Scenario questions

> [!question]- Q15. A critical production bug is reported just before a release ⭐
> **In simple words:** **contain, communicate, correct, prevent.**
>
> 1. **Clarify severity:** how many users, data loss, money impact?
> 2. **Communicate immediately** to the lead / stakeholders. Don't fix silently.
> 3. **Contain:** decide with the lead between **hotfix, rollback, feature-flag off, or delay the release**.
> 4. **Correct:** reproduce, check **CloudWatch logs**, fix **with a test** for that case.
> 5. **Prevent:** short root-cause note; add a test / alarm so it can't repeat.
>
> **Diagram:**
> ```mermaid
> flowchart TD
>   B["Bug reported"] --> S{"Critical?"}
>   S -- "yes" --> C["Tell lead now"] --> D{"Quick safe fix?"}
>   D -- "yes" --> HF["Hotfix + test"]
>   D -- "no" --> RB["Rollback / flag off / delay release"]
>   S -- "no" --> BL["Log it, fix in next sprint"]
>   HF --> RCA["Root cause + prevention"]
>   RB --> RCA
> ```
> 💡 **Hack:** **feature flags** (your resume!) make "turn it off" possible without a redeploy.

> [!question]- Q16. You realise you can't meet a deadline ⭐
> **In simple words:** **bad news early is information; bad news late is a failure.**
>
> - Raise it **as soon as you know**, with data: done / left / blocker.
> - Offer **options**: cut scope (MVP first), get help, move the date.
> - Let the lead / PO decide, then **deliver what you committed**.
>
> 💡 **Hack:** **"Early, with data, with options."**

> [!question]- Q17. How do you handle multiple priorities at once? ⭐
> - List everything, rank by **impact × urgency** (Eisenhower matrix)
> - When two things are "top priority", **ask the lead / PO** to choose
> - Block focus time, keep the **Azure DevOps board** updated
>
> **Diagram:**
> ```mermaid
> quadrantChart
>   title Eisenhower matrix
>   x-axis Not urgent --> Urgent
>   y-axis Not important --> Important
>   quadrant-1 Do now
>   quadrant-2 Schedule
>   quadrant-3 Drop
>   quadrant-4 Delegate
>   Prod bug: [0.9, 0.9]
>   Upgrade plan: [0.2, 0.8]
>   Status mail: [0.8, 0.25]
>   Nice-to-have: [0.15, 0.15]
> ```
> 💡 **Hack:** **"Do, Schedule, Delegate, Drop."**

> [!question]- Q18. You disagree with a senior on a technical approach
> 1. **Listen** to understand their reasons.
> 2. Compare options **with data**: a quick POC, performance numbers, maintainability.
> 3. Agree on the **goal**, then decide together, or let the lead / architect decide.
> 4. Once decided, **commit fully**, even if it wasn't your idea.
>
> 💡 **Hack:** **"Disagree and commit."** Facts, not ego.

> [!question]- Q19. A teammate isn't contributing or keeps missing deadlines
> Talk **privately** first: ask what's blocking them, offer help (pairing, clearer tasks). If it continues and hurts delivery, raise it with the lead **factually**. Never blame in public.
>
> 💡 **Hack:** **"Private, helpful, then factual."**

> [!question]- Q20. The client changes requirements in the middle of a sprint
> Understand the **why** → assess impact on scope and dates → discuss with the PO → swap with lower-priority work or plan it for the next sprint → document the decision.
>
> 💡 **Hack:** **"Change is fine. Unmanaged change isn't."**

> [!question]- Q21. You get a task in a technology you've never used, with a tight deadline
> Point to proof: *"That happened with Step Functions and with MCP."* Approach: **official docs → small POC → ask an expert → build in small steps → share what I learned.**

> [!question]- Q22. The client is unhappy with your team's delivery
> Listen fully and **don't get defensive** → acknowledge → find the real issue (quality? speed? communication?) → agree on a concrete fix and a check-in date → over-communicate until trust is back.

> [!question]- Q23. Are you comfortable working late or on weekends during releases? ⭐
> > "Yes, during critical releases or production issues that's part of the job. Day to day I prefer planning well so it's the exception, not the routine."

> [!question]- Q24. How do you ensure code quality?
> Code reviews on every PR, **Jest** unit tests + **Playwright** E2E, lint rules and **Nx module boundaries**, **SCA** scans, **feature flags** for safe rollout, and a **CI coverage gate**. Proof: **20% → 78% coverage**.
>
> 💡 **Hack:** **"Review, Test, Lint, Scan, Flag, Gate."**

> [!question]- Q25. How do you mentor juniors or onboard new members?
> Pair programming on the first tickets, good first issues, documentation (the **AI spec agent** produces specs they can read), and PR reviews that **explain why**, not just what to change.

## Process & engineering judgement

> [!question]- Q26. Explain Agile / Scrum and your ceremonies
> **In simple words:** work in short cycles (**sprints**, usually 2 weeks), show working software at the end of each, and improve the process every time.
>
> **Diagram:**
> ```mermaid
> flowchart LR
>   PB["Product backlog"] --> SP["Sprint planning"]
>   SP --> SB["Sprint backlog"]
>   SB --> DS["Daily stand-up<br/>15 min, every day"]
>   DS --> INC["Working increment"]
>   INC --> RV["Sprint review / demo"]
>   RV --> RT["Retrospective"]
>   RT --> SP
>   PB -. "refinement" .-> PB
> ```
> **Roles:** Product Owner (what), Scrum Master (process), Dev team (how). **Tool:** Azure DevOps boards.
>
> 💡 **Hack:** **"Plan, Stand, Refine, Review, Retro."**

> [!question]- Q27. How do you estimate a task?
> Break it into small sub-tasks → compare with similar past work (**story points**, planning poker) → add buffer for testing and unknowns → re-estimate when you learn something new.
>
> 💡 **Hack:** **relative, not absolute**: "this is about twice that other story".

> [!question]- Q28. REST vs OData vs GraphQL: when would you choose which?
> | | Best for | Watch out |
> |---|---|---|
> | **REST** | simple CRUD, caching, most APIs | many endpoints for many screens |
> | **OData** | enterprise / reporting UIs over relational data (**your work**) | heavier, mostly Microsoft ecosystem |
> | **GraphQL** | many different clients needing different shapes | caching, security, N+1 queries |
>
> 💡 **Hack:** **REST = fixed menu, OData = menu with filters, GraphQL = build your own plate.**

> [!question]- Q29. How would you design the front end of a large enterprise app?
> Nx monorepo with **feature libraries + a shared UI library**; lazy-loaded routes; **NgRx (or a signal store)** for shared state, local state in components; typed API models shared across apps; **interceptors** for auth and errors; **OnPush** everywhere; a design system; CI with **affected-only** builds and tests.
>
> **Diagram:**
> ```mermaid
> flowchart TD
>   SH["Shell app<br/>routing · auth · layout"] --> F1["feature: pricing (lazy)"]
>   SH --> F2["feature: reports (lazy)"]
>   F1 --> UI["shared/ui"]
>   F2 --> UI
>   F1 --> DA["shared/data-access<br/>API services + NgRx"]
>   F2 --> DA
>   DA --> UT["shared/utils"]
> ```
